**Author:** Alexander Booth
**Date:** July 2026

## Overview

Orientation introduces the Faculty Director, **Paul McDonagh-Smith**, a senior lecturer in information technology at the MIT Sloan School of Management, and frames the program's overall arc. The two videos set up why Agentic AI is a distinct leadership challenge, not just a new tool, and preview the three-module journey: **explore** what Agentic AI is and where it creates value (Module 1), **implement** it through agent architecture and orchestration (Module 2), and **scale** it responsibly through governance (Module 3).

## Why This Module Matters

Before learners evaluate any framework or design any workflow, they need a shared mental model of what has actually changed in AI, and why it changes the leadership question. Orientation makes the case that the shift from reactive AI (prompt in, response out) to **Agentic AI** (systems that plan, use tools, pursue multistep goals, and act autonomously) turns "how do we use AI?" into "how do we lead when AI can act autonomously?" Every subsequent module builds on this reframing, so getting it right here is what makes the rest of the program's frameworks land as leadership tools rather than technical trivia.

## What This Module Covers

1. **The shift from reactive to agentic AI.** For several years, AI has been reactive: it waits for a prompt and responds. Agentic AI is different — it plans, uses tools, pursues goals across multiple steps, makes decisions along the way, and acts, already in production today across customer service, supply chain operations, compliance monitoring, and code generation.

2. **A new leadership question.** Because agentic systems act autonomously rather than simply respond, the central question for leaders shifts from "how do we use AI?" to "how do we lead when AI can act autonomously?" This reframes Agentic AI as a leadership and management challenge, not only a technology challenge.

3. **The three-module program arc.** The program moves through a deliberate, cumulative sequence:
   - **Module 1 (Explore):** What Agentic AI actually is, positioned on the broader AI continuum from 1950s rule-based systems through machine learning, deep learning, and generative AI to today's proactive agents. Covers what distinguishes an agent from a chatbot (planning, tool use, multistep goal pursuit, autonomy) and where agentic systems create genuine organizational value versus where constraints remain.
   - **Module 2 (Implement):** How to operationalize agentic systems — the scaffolding that turns a large language model into a reliable, goal-directed agent; the agent loop (plan, act, observe, iterate); and why and how organizations move from single agents to multi-agent systems requiring orchestration. Grounded in applied workflows: customer-facing agents, operational and supply chain agents, risk and compliance agents.
   - **Module 3 (Scale):** How to move from a successful pilot to enterprise-scale deployment without losing control — confronting governance, risk, and trust questions such as security vulnerabilities, error propagation, and accountability gaps, and designing safeguards that earn the trust that scaling demands.

4. **What learners will leave with.** Not a vendor evaluation or technology shopping list, but a strategic deployment playbook — phased, governed, and grounded in a credible ROI narrative — plus what the Faculty Director calls an **"agentic instinct"**: the ability to look at a workflow or decision chain and see where autonomous agents could create value, where they'd create risk, and what responsible deployment would actually take. This instinct is built by working through design decisions, not by reading alone, and is supported by frameworks for evaluating agent capabilities, mapping workflows, assessing risks, and designing governance, plus perspectives from MIT faculty and industry practitioners.

5. **Program policies and Honor Code.** The Honor Code and Program Policies document (delivered via GetSmarter) sets the ground rules participants agree to for the program: general conduct expectations, discussion forum rules and sanctions, intellectual property and confidentiality guidance, personal information requirements, academic integrity and the plagiarism policy (including the plagiarism declaration required on assessments), the extension request process, faulty submission handling, and the result query process.

## Key Takeaways

* **Agentic AI acts, it doesn't just respond** — it plans, uses tools, pursues multistep goals, and makes decisions autonomously, already deployed across real operational workflows.
* The core leadership question is no longer "how do we use AI?" but **"how do we lead when AI can act autonomously?"**
* The program follows a deliberate **explore → implement → scale** arc: what Agentic AI is and where it creates value, then how to architect and orchestrate it, then how to govern it at enterprise scale.
* The goal is an **agentic instinct** — the ability to spot where autonomous agents create value or risk in a real workflow — built through design work, not passive reading.
* Participants are bound by the **Honor Code and Program Policies**, covering conduct, academic integrity, plagiarism, and the extension and grading query processes.
